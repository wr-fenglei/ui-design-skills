# App Icon Design Skill

## Purpose

Analyze, explore, and refine diverse, coherent app-icon directions using the bundled references.

Identify and deliberately vary the design logic itself: metaphor, silhouette, composition, dimensionality, material, color, spatial behavior, and emotional tone.

## Core principle

**Reference images calibrate the design space; they are not templates to copy.**

When exploring:
1. Expand broadly before converging.
2. Make directions structurally different, not merely recolored.
3. Preserve a clear design thesis for every direction.
4. Use reference images to judge diversity, quality, and visual distance.

## Required analysis dimensions

Before generating or refining any icon, analyze it across all of these dimensions:

### 1. Design metaphor
What idea or mental model drives the design?
Examples: portal, layered intelligence, flow, connection, signal, orbit, tool, container, dialogue, transformation, structure, growth.

### 2. Silhouette language
What makes the shape recognizable at thumbnail size?
Consider:
- hard geometric vs organic
- closed vs open
- symmetric vs asymmetric
- monolithic vs fragmented
- centered vs directional
- dense vs sparse
- positive shape vs negative-space construction

### 3. Composition
Describe the actual spatial system:
- axis and balance
- dominant mass
- hierarchy
- cropping
- overlap
- internal voids
- directionality
- rhythm/repetition
- foreground/background relationship

### 4. Color system
Do not describe color only by names. Explain:
- temperature
- contrast strategy
- saturation
- number of dominant hues
- background/foreground relationship
- accent behavior
- whether color is structural, semantic, atmospheric, or merely decorative

### 5. Material language
Identify the perceived material and surface behavior:
- flat ink
- paper
- glass
- gel
- ceramic
- stone
- metal
- plastic
- light
- pixel/display
- fabric
- translucent film
- matte vs glossy
- hard vs soft

### 6. Dimensionality
Classify the spatial logic:
- strict 2D
- 2.5D
- sculptural 3D
- volumetric light
- layered transparency
- recessed/embossed
- impossible geometry
- perspective or orthographic

### 7. Edge and corner language
Track:
- sharp vs rounded
- constant vs variable radius
- cut corners
- chamfers
- soft deformation
- pixel steps
- folded edges
- contour tension

### 8. Light and shadow
If present, explain:
- light direction
- softness
- depth cue
- ambient occlusion
- rim light
- glow
- cast shadow
- whether lighting is illustrative, physical, or symbolic

### 9. Texture and detail density
Specify:
- clean vector
- grain
- noise
- speckle
- microtexture
- grid
- scanline
- brushed surface
- low/high visual frequency

### 10. Emotional tone
Examples:
- precise
- serious
- playful
- editorial
- architectural
- natural
- futuristic
- quiet
- premium
- mechanical
- friendly
- experimental

### 11. Motion implication
Even for static icons, ask whether the form implies:
- expansion
- rotation
- flow
- folding
- entering
- emergence
- assembly
- signal propagation
- breathing
- compression

### 12. Brand behavior
Ask what remains invariant across refinements and variants of the icon:
- one signature shape?
- a specific cut?
- a material?
- a spatial relationship?
- a contrast rule?
- a recurring motion?
- a distinctive negative-space device?

This invariant is more important than copying the exact icon outline.

## Workflow — Generate icon directions

### Step 1: Define the product signal
Extract only the minimum semantic requirements from the product:
- what the product does
- what emotional quality it should project
- what clichés should be avoided

Do not overfit icon concepts to literal product features.

### Step 2: Construct a wide design space
Create directions that vary across multiple axes at once.

Useful direction families:
- geometric / negative-space
- constructivist / editorial
- organic / biomorphic
- pixel / signal / digital
- architectural / object-like
- celestial / atmospheric
- folded paper / layered planes
- luminous / spectral
- typographic abstraction
- modular systems
- tactile material studies
- minimal symbolic marks

Do not make six variants that share the same composition and change only color/material.

### Step 3: Describe each direction before generating
For every candidate, write a short design thesis covering:
- metaphor
- silhouette
- composition
- color logic
- material
- dimensionality
- emotional tone

### Step 4: Generate in batches
Prefer broad batches early. Compare candidates for **design distance**, not just beauty.

### Step 5: Evaluate
Reject directions that are:
- too similar to another candidate
- dependent on tiny detail
- generic AI-gradient blobs without a structural idea
- visually attractive but conceptually weak
- difficult to recognize at small size
- obvious copies of references

### Step 6: Refine selected directions
Once a direction is selected, refine shape logic, spacing, color, material, and rendering without destroying the original thesis.

## Quality checklist

Before finalizing, verify:

- Is the direction meaningfully different from neighboring concepts?
- Can the icon still read at small size?
- Is there one clear visual thesis?
- Are color and material supporting the thesis rather than substituting for it?
- Is negative space deliberate?
